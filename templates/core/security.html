Security, stated plainly

A vault earns trust by being specific about what it does and does not do.

What VeriVault does

  • Private file storage. Documents are saved outside the public web root. Every download passes an ownership or share check.
  • Integrity fingerprints. A SHA-256 hash is recorded on upload and for every version, and can be rechecked at any time.
  • Two-factor authentication. Optional authenticator-app codes (TOTP, RFC 6238) on sign-in.
  • Re-authentication. Sensitive and critical documents ask for your password before download.
  • Brute-force throttling. Repeated failed sign-ins from the same address are temporarily blocked.
  • Hardened defaults. Content-Security-Policy, secure cookies, CSRF protection, blocked executable uploads and safe inline preview rules.
  • Audit trail. Views, downloads, shares, verifications and sign-ins are recorded with time and IP address.

What it does not do

  • It does not add its own encryption at rest. Use full-disk encryption on the server that hosts it.
  • It does not replace HTTPS. Serve it over TLS in any real deployment.
  • It is not a legal e-signature or notarisation service. A certificate proves a file is unchanged since upload, not that its contents are true.
